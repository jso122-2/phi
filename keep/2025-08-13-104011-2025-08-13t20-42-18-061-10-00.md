What do you do when you have to train a machine learning pipeline but only have 13 pieces of data?

During a recent contract, I designed and built a machine learning pipeline to format and parse documents into internal templates — a one-click execution tool.

Sounds simple enough. But I was initially given just 13 pieces of training data.
For anyone who works with ML, that’s basically nothing. Many would say “I need more data” or “It’s not possible.”

Here’s the thing: in ML, data is your budget. For a creative agency, it’s like funding — the more you have, the more resources you can bring to bear. And a model can only work with what it’s trained on. If it hasn’t seen it, it doesn’t know it exists.

So instead of making this my client’s problem, I made it mine.
I extracted every nuance, every style cue, every subtle pattern from those 13 examples. Then I generated 500 synthetic “style copies” — not duplicates, but high-fidelity imitations indistinguishable to the model.
With mutations, that dataset grew to 5,000 examples.

From there, I implemented custom FAISS matrices and smart k-kerneling to create a sentence encoder that was bulletproof. The result: document formatting and parsing in under 200ms latency.

The tool was built, tested, and deployed.
Lesson? As a contractor, it’s never “I can’t deliver.” If the data (or resources) aren’t there, make them.
